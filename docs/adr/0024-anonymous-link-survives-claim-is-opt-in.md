# Anonymous link on shared artifacts survives; identity is opt-in via claim

The public link to any artifact — the workout handout at `/h/{planId}`, the workout player at `/p/{planId}`, and every future `kind` registered in `plan_artifacts` (see [ADR 0022](0022-plan-artifacts-abstraction-before-reel.md)) — opens **anonymously**. No auth wall, no login prompt, no silent identity token minted into a cookie. The recipient lands on the artifact and can immediately consume it, exactly the way they can today via `get_plan_full` ([`web-player/api.js`](../../web-player/api.js)). On top of that anonymous surface we layer a soft, dismissible **claim chip** that invites the recipient to sign up with a magic-link email; on success the claim flow attaches the *current plan* (the page already knows its `plan_id` from the URL) to the newly-created account, and from that moment forward the recipient is an identified client linked to the practitioner's existing `clients` row (with its `video_consent` jsonb already populated by the practitioner-proxy surface). The chip escalates on return visits — slightly more prominent, slightly more copy — but **never becomes a modal, never becomes a wall, never blocks the exercises** (R-01, `CLAUDE.md`). Auth is **magic-link only for now**; Apple Sign-In is scaffolded but blocked on the Developer Program activation, Google is parked on the nonce-mismatch issue documented in [`docs/BACKLOG_GOOGLE_SIGNIN.md`](../BACKLOG_GOOGLE_SIGNIN.md), and adding either is a follow-up wave once the Reader-App-safe email flow has bedded in. Recipients who never claim stay genuinely anonymous: analytics is session-aggregate only (Wave 17 already shipped this surface — `plan_analytics_events` keyed by anonymous `client_sessions`, not by a recipient identity), no email is collected, no device fingerprint is stored against the plan. Named adherence attribution starts **claim-forward only** — there is no retroactive de-anonymisation of pre-claim sessions, even after the same recipient subsequently claims, because pre-claim sessions weren't consented to be named and we are not in the business of inferring identity backwards. This is the locked answer to decision #11 in the artifact-system design log and the de-facto contract every future artifact kind inherits.

## Considered Options

We considered **requiring login on every shared link** — gate `/h/{planId}` and `/p/{planId}` behind a sign-in screen, mint identified `clients` rows for every recipient. This was the proposed alternative and was rejected on three grounds. First, **adherence**: homefit's validated thesis is that visual plans plus low friction roughly double compliance ([`docs/MARKET_RESEARCH.md`](../MARKET_RESEARCH.md) — adherence rises from 38% to 77% with visual instructions). Mandatory login at the door breaks the low-friction half of that thesis directly. The realistic recipient population includes sore-knee patients on a borrowed phone, low-tech or older patients who don't keep a working email password, and patients who would rather just do the exercises than create another account; every one of them is a churn point we're voluntarily introducing right where the value is highest. Second, **POPIA**: today's model is zero-recipient-PII by default — line drawings de-identify the captured exercises ([ADR 0002](0002-on-device-line-drawing-as-core-ip.md)), anonymous reads happen through the `get_plan_full` SECURITY DEFINER RPC, and no recipient email is collected unless they affirmatively claim. Mandatory login moves every recipient into the identified bucket and inflates our data-subject obligations across the board (consent records, deletion-request scope, breach-notification surface) for the population that wasn't asking to be identified in the first place. Third, **complexity relocation, not reduction**: mandatory login is sometimes framed as "simplifies downstream features" — claim-by-name attribution, anchored client/practitioner messaging, multi-device sync of completion state. But it doesn't remove that complexity, it relocates it into auth and onboarding. The current model lets us ship the same downstream features *gated by claim*, narrower in scope but identical in capability — and the claim-gated version is the one the parked anchored-comms work was already designed around. We also considered **a silent identity token** minted on first open (a UUID cookie stamped to the device, attaching the recipient to the plan without an explicit account). Rejected: it identifies non-claimers without consent, reintroduces the POPIA problem the anonymous-by-default model was designed around, and is the kind of dark-pattern silent attribution the [`feedback_ios_reader_app.md`](../../.claude/projects/-Users-chm-dev-TrainMe/memory/feedback_ios_reader_app.md) instinct argues against on the mobile surface — extending the same instinct to web. We considered a **soft modal "would you like to claim?" overlay** on first load instead of the inline chip. Rejected: that is a modal under any reasonable definition (it intercepts attention before the recipient can interact with the content), it violates R-01 directly, and the conversion data from other adherence-style products is consistent that persistent-but-quiet chips outperform hard-CTAs over the lifetime of the relationship — a hard CTA wins the first impression and loses the second visit. Finally we considered **mandatory login only on the first visit** as a one-time tax. Rejected for the same adherence reason as the always-on variant: the friction lands precisely at the moment of highest value, when the practitioner has just shared the link and the recipient is about to start the exercises. We accept three explicit trade-offs in exchange for keeping the link anonymous: claim-conversion will be lower than mandatory-login would force, anchored-comms features (parked in the artifact-system design doc) will be claim-gated by nature and pre-claim recipients cannot participate, and practitioners get the named adherence view only for clients who have claimed — pre-claim recipients show up in the aggregate analytics surface but never in named per-client reports. All three are acceptable; the adherence and POPIA wins are the load-bearing reasons we exist as a product the way we do.
