# Pricing is per artifact kind; the floor is free

Each artifact kind in `plan_artifacts` (ADR 0022) carries its own price, configured at the registry level and summed at publish time. **Workout handout** (`kind='handout'`, the free interactive page at `session.homefit.studio/h/{uuid}`) is priced at **0 credits**. **Workout player** (`kind='plan_url'`, the existing paid surface at `session.homefit.studio/p/{uuid}`) retains the duration-based price from ADR 0007 unchanged: 1 credit for plans ≤ 75 minutes of estimated duration, 2 credits above. **Reel** and **AI reel** sit at premium pricing TBD; **poster** and **calendar** at free or token-priced, also TBD. The publish gate (mockup at `docs/design/mockups/2026-05-26-publish-gate.html`) is a multi-select checklist (locked decision #21) — the practitioner picks which kinds to mint for this plan, nothing pre-checked, with a live running total. Confirm sums the selected paid kinds, calls `consume_credit(p_practice_id, p_plan_id, total_credits)` once, and upserts every chosen `plan_artifacts` row in the same transaction alongside `plan_issuances`. The existing compensating-refund pattern (see `supabase/archive/schema_milestone_e_revoke_credit_ledger_writes.sql`) carries over unchanged — a materialised-render failure (reel transcode error, AI generation timeout) writes a refund row tied to the originating `consume_credit` event. Per-kind price is debited **once at first enablement**, not re-charged on every plan edit or version bump; the `UNIQUE (plan_id, kind)` constraint on `plan_artifacts` guards this, and already-enabled kinds render as Live in the gate and are no-ops if re-ticked. The free floor is deliberate (locked decision #4): the handout is the funnel artifact that earns the practitioner referral rebates via the QR code's `?ref={code}` attribution, so making it free aligns the practitioner's growth incentive with homefit's — both want the handout in as many client hands as possible. The publish-flow extension lives in `app/lib/services/upload_service.dart` (which currently calls `consume_credit` and upserts the implicit single-kind row); the multi-kind atomic publish replaces the single `consume_credit` call with a registry-driven sum and folds the artifact upserts into the same transaction as `plan_issuances`. The artifact-system design doc at `docs/ARTIFACT_SYSTEM.md` is the source of truth for kind enumeration, pricing constants, and the renderer interface.

## Considered Options

We considered **a flat per-plan price** (one credit per plan, regardless of how many artifacts the practitioner mints). Rejected because it forecloses the free-floor strategy: a practitioner who only wants to share the handout has to spend a credit to do so, which converts a funnel artifact into a paywalled one and breaks the referral attribution loop before it can spin. The flat model also becomes incoherent in a multi-kind world — the credit is tied to the plan but the artifacts have meaningfully different render costs (a computed `get_plan_full` read for the handout vs. a beat-matched 9:16 transcode for the reel) and meaningfully different consumer value. Charging the same price for both either over-prices the funnel or under-prices the reel.

We considered **per-plan price with optional add-on artifact prices** (1 credit for plan + N credits for premium kinds). Rejected as a halfway house — it still gates the free floor behind the plan-credit, which preserves the funnel-paywalling problem above. The clean version of the rule is "the handout is 0 credits, plain stop"; introducing a base price for the plan itself is a tax on every artifact the practitioner mints, including the one we want frictionless.

We considered **no free floor, every artifact priced** (handout at e.g. 0.1 credits, requiring `credit_ledger.credits` to widen to `numeric` from the current integer). Rejected because micro-pricing the funnel artifact defeats its purpose. The funnel needs to be frictionless; a 0.1-credit price introduces accounting friction without meaningful revenue. Locked decision #4 records this explicitly — the free floor is intentional, and the funnel job (driving signups via the QR-attributed referral link on every handout) is what justifies it.

We considered **a subscription model for paid artifacts** (e.g. an unlimited-publish-month at R200 instead of per-publish). Rejected per ADR 0007's reasoning on subscription friction for SA-based practitioners, plus the cohesion with ADR 0021's credit-denominated Safe Mode subscription: keeping every priced surface on the same credit-debit pattern keeps the referral rebate flywheel spinning (5% lifetime rebate on every referee purchase, see `supabase/archive/schema_milestone_m_credit_model.sql`). A separate subscription channel would silo revenue and break the loop. The credit-denominated subscription pattern from ADR 0021 is the canonical template for any future recurring revenue here.

We considered **charging once per plan_url lifetime and free re-publishes** as if it were an alternative. It is not — it is the rule, applied per kind. The ADR records it explicitly so the implementation cannot drift: per-kind first-enablement triggers the debit, the `plan_artifacts` row stays live and tracks the plan thereafter, and re-ticking a Live kind in the publish gate is a UI no-op. The edge case worth flagging for the implementation PR is plan deletion + recreation: a practitioner who deletes a plan and re-publishes the same URL would technically re-trigger the charge if the `plan_artifacts` rows are cascade-deleted with the plan. The implementation either preserves the artifact rows across plan soft-delete (the safer path — `plans.deleted_at IS NOT NULL` plus a matching `plan_artifacts` row means "previously paid, restore for free") or surfaces an explicit warning in the recycle-bin restore flow. The trade-off worth acknowledging on the free floor: homefit eats the render and storage cost of every published handout. The cost is approximately zero in practice — the handout is a computed page (no materialised storage, just a `get_plan_full` read at request time against data already present in `plans` and `exercises`), so marginal cost is the Vercel request and the Supabase read, both of which the practitioner's existing plan-URL traffic already incurs. Premium pricing for the reel is left TBD on purpose; we avoid pre-committing to a number we will likely revise once the render infrastructure exists and the per-render cost is measurable.
