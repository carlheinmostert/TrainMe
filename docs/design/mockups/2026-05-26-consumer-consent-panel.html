Consent is at practice grain — one section per linked practice, not per-practitioner — because practitioners in one practice share the client record. Anatomy

Practice-grain, per-relationship

MV One card per linked practice, not per-practitioner — practitioners inside one practice share the client record. Not global either: you can be more open with one practice than another. The shape maps cleanly to rendering — every plan belongs to exactly one practice, so an artifact reads the matching relationship's consent. Per-relationship consent and per-plan gating are the same thing.

Line is locked ON

Line drawing is de-identified by the pipeline (POPIA-compliant by design) — consent can't be withdrawn because there's nothing identifying to withdraw. It's always available across every practice. Shown as a coral-filled, non-tappable pill paired with a small lock glyph next to the name to make the why visible. Distinct from "OFF" (empty) and "ON" (coral, tappable).

Inherit on claim, then your own

Pre-claim the practitioner sets these by proxy (on clients.video_consent). On claim the consumer's settings inherit the practitioner's current state — continuity, nothing visibly changes. The coral first-visit chip reads "here's what's currently shared — you can change it" so the inherited state is owned, not surprising. After the first edit the consumer owns it; the practitioner only sees what's off.

Transparency, not lock-out

On the practitioner side they see "Analytics OFF for this client" (or any toggle the consumer turned off) — so they understand why session stats are missing or why the original-colour treatment isn't rendering. They can't override or fake it. No clinical carve-out — but the visibility means the consumer staying in control doesn't break the practitioner's mental model.

Why this shape

One card per practice, six toggles each. The six rows are the consent matrix: line drawing (locked), B&W, original, profile photo, face fingerprint, workout stats. Same six everywhere — only the values differ. Predictable shape across cards is what makes the "more open here, stricter there" pattern legible.

Plan count anchors the relationship. "1 plan from this practice" / "2 plans" grounds the abstract toggle row in the concrete artifacts it affects. "Strictest setting" on Tariro's card reads as a gentle tell that this is the most-locked-down practice — without ever pricing the consumer's choice.

"Stop all stats" master at the foot. One-tap to flip Workout stats OFF across every practice. The relationship structure stays intact; the leverage is over the analytics surface only — the place where over-sharing is most invisible and easiest to fix in one motion.

No "delete relationship" button. Leaving a practice is a real-world conversation, not a UI primitive. The footer says so explicitly so the consumer doesn't hunt for a kill switch that would corrupt the practitioner's records.
