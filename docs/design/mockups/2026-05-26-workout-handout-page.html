What the brand-skin actually does

Side-by-side anatomy

Default page (left) — every plan ships with this look. The header carries the homefit.studio lockup; coral is the accent across the claim chip, treatment toggle active state, circuit border, and footer seal.
Brand-skin page (right) — when the practitioner is on the ~2 cr/mo brand-skin subscription, the same plan renders with their identity. The homefit.studio wordmark in the header is replaced by the practitioner's own lockup (here: Range + the range-of-motion glyph + "movement studio" tag); the accent flips from coral to the practitioner's brand colour (here: red) across the claim chip, treatment toggle, circuit border. The byline updates to "from Margaret Vorster · Range Movement".
The footer seal stays coral. Look at the bottom of the right-hand page: the "powered by homefit.studio" mark + QR are still in homefit coral, not red. The seal is the one part the brand-skin cannot repaint — that's the locked decision (#23). The contrast between a red-skinned page and a coral seal is the load-bearing visual proof that homefit is never white-labeled away.

Why a permanent seal is a feature, not a tax

Tri-party deal. The seal earns its place by working for three different audiences at once. The patient reads it as a credibility signal ("my practitioner runs on a real, auditable system" — like an "as seen on" mark). The practitioner gets their logo in the dominant top position (the brand-skin is what they're paying for) plus the platform's credibility mark in the footer. homefit gets a permanent funnel.

The QR carries ?ref={code}. The QR's link attribution is the practitioner's referral code — but the QR's destination is always the plan itself, not a signup page. So a recipient scanning the seal lands on the plan with timers, and any future signup is attributed to the practitioner who showed them the seal. The seal can earn the practitioner referral rebates — which is the strongest reason they're happy to keep it.

Lapse → revert. The brand-skin is a live property: if the subscription lapses, every artifact already shared and sitting in clients' phones live-reverts to the default homefit look. Renewal pressure intended; per-publish-date retention deliberately rejected — too much complexity for too little upside.

Where the brand pieces come from

Sourced from the existing portal public profile. The practitioner's logo + accent colour + practice name + contact already live on the public profile that shipped with the self-trainer wave on staging. Brand-skin is not a data-capture feature — it's a positioning feature: it tells the renderer to surface those existing fields here, in this slot.

Open question. The exact field-by-field mapping for each artifact type is open question #9 in the design doc. For the handout, the spots are clear: header lockup, accent colour, byline practice name. Poster and reel will need their own positioning decisions.

Behaviour shared with the single-variant view

Claim chip behaviour — coral-tinted (or skin-tinted), dismissible, quiet first visit, subtle pulse on third-visit-onward, never a modal/wall (R-01), magic-link sign-in attaches the current plan to the new account.

Treatment + consent — Line is always available (de-identified by the pipeline); B&W and Original are consent-gated per the client's video_consent. Same rule as the player (locked decision #10) — no "higher bar for print".

Version stamp on print. The footer "v3 · 26 May 2026 14:32" survives onto paper, so a printed handout traces back to a specific publish. The artifact is live; a printed sheet is frozen — the stamp tells you when it froze.
