Two intents, one sheet

Sharing splits the moment a practitioner decides what they need from a client — a fast blast that reaches them on WhatsApp, or a captured email that anchors the engagement rail. The sheet asks the real question with one tap.

1Two paths, two intents

›
Share link is the blast — any channel, no contact captured. WhatsApp dominates per market research (84% of SA healthcare workers use it for work), so the OS sheet is the default path most practitioners will reach for.
›
Send by email is the anchor — captures the address for the engagement rail. The split asks the practitioner the real question: WhatsApp or email?

2Why email is in here at all

›
Email is the engagement rail’s filler when the client doesn’t claim. The typed address is what we send save your plan nudges to.
›
Verified claim emails supersede the typed one (POPIA data-minimization). Open question: show claimed · linked status only, or expose the verified address to the practitioner?

3The “no surprises” copy

›
Both CTAs name what they do. Send · 1 email commits a single branded send; the OS sheet labels itself per its own conventions (the practitioner picks the channel from there). No standalone Share button — the verb without a noun is the ambiguity we’re trying to retire.

4What happens to the typed email

›
When a verified claim email later attaches to the plan, the typed pre-claim address is deleted. Pre-claim is engagement-rail scaffolding only; post-claim, the verified one is the source of truth.
›
Surfacing this commitment in the legend (and possibly inline on the sheet as the asterisked note) lets the practitioner trust the capture — we’re not building a shadow contact list, we’re bridging until the client speaks for themselves.
